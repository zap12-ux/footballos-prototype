## What changed in the protocol

- **Success criteria.** Phase 0 fixes four targets. At least 80% of role tasks including W1 must be completed unaided. There must be zero findings of silently discarded coach input. Every AI output encountered during the workflow must lead to supporting evidence in at most two interactions. The adoption target is an unconditional `yes` to using the product during the season. Each report opens with the four measured results. This applies Perri’s outcome-based definition of done (2018, ch. 19).
- **Weekly work.** W1 follows one Saturday grading asset through handoff, one Sunday scouting and planning decision through Monday install and approval, or one Sunday head-coach staff check through parent visibility. Each stage records its own actions, evidence and friction. The scenario draws on Perri’s guidance about observing people in their setting (2018, ch. 24). Here the setting and role are simulated.
- **Switching and demand.** Q1–Q10 keep their original wording and order. Q11–Q13 ask what could displace the current tools, whether the coach would use the product voluntarily, and which single season outcome would show value (Cagan, 2017, ch. 51).
- **Weekly cost and decision ownership.** Findings retain severity and add an urgency rating, a value rating, a weekly rationale, a MoSCoW priority and the stakeholder who could veto the affected decision. The kit assigns high, medium and low the weights 3, 2 and 1, then ranks by urgency × value. Ties go to higher urgency, then lower numeric finding ID. The score is an ordinal convention for this review. Perri (2018, ch. 19) informs outcome prioritization, Cagan (2017, ch. 61) and Cagan and Jones (2020) inform decision ownership, and Javed (2025) discusses stakeholder engagement and MoSCoW.
- **Evidence and learnings.** The AI inventory records which output was inspected, what actions reached its evidence and whether the destination supported the claim. An incomplete inventory or an unverified output cannot earn the evidence goal. This applies Huyen’s (2022) emphasis on evaluating actual system behavior in context. Each report closes with what worked, what did not and what to try next (Cagan, 2017, ch. 62).

Run a role using the shared protocol. For round 2, recheck every prior finding using the same role and include the previous and current MoSCoW, cost-of-delay score and rank in the comparison.
