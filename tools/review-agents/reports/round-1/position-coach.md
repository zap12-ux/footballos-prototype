## Interview answers

**1. First impression in one sentence.**

I could tell who owned the film decisions from the landing page, and I could see the gap between a proposed cut and a coach’s review as soon as I opened Stonebrook.

**2. Did the product match what the landing page led you to expect? Where not?**

The proposal and correction history matched my expectation. The promise that I could check the frames did not hold for the workspace claims I opened. The cell took me to a short credited clip, and my posted handoff disappeared on refresh.

**3. Walk me through your normal week. Where would this fit, and where does it fight your routine?**

I spend eight to ten hours most weeks tagging snaps, checking assignments and pulling practice clips. On Saturday I run Hudl alongside a grading sheet. The proposal queue could fit before I enter a grade, and the Monday drill belongs in the unit plan. Typing the game, cut and time a second time fights that routine.

**4. What confused you? What did you have to figure out?**

“Three proposed” with “zero in the review queue” made me wonder which list I was supposed to work. The same upload had three queued after I reopened it. I also had to open an apparently skipped personnel cell to find out that “already reviewed” included empty entries.

**5. Where did you trust the AI's output, where did you not, and what made the difference?**

I trusted the authored explanation for withholding a grade when the camera left the interior. I trusted my own reason still being visible in a revision while the film page remained open. I could not trust the Ace or run proposal on Westmoor when its timestamp played a different snap, or assign the blind-play breakdown to the guard from the defender’s position alone.

**6. What would you stop using it over?**

I would stop when a refresh erases a Saturday decision that already said it was saved. I saw both the play 10 revision and the coach-facing comment disappear.

**7. What would you change first? Second?**

First, keep the correction and the coordinator’s comment across refresh and make pending drafts safe while I check a clip. Second, make the cited cut and the handoff open the same original snap.

**8. What should they not touch?**

Keep the blind grade before revealing the candidate, the separate player-ID and grade confidence labels, and the requirement to give a reason for No Grade. Keep the practice focus telling me which reviewed plays changed it.

**9. Would you use this during the season? Would you recommend it to your staff? Answer yes, no or conditional, with the condition.**

No for my in-season grading, and no for a staff rollout of this version. I could exercise the process, but I could not give the coordinator a correction that would still be there after reload.

**10. Anything else.**

The comment I wanted on play 10 was a request for an end-zone copy before we called the strength. Leaving that formation open is a useful coaching decision and it needs the same protection as a positive tag.

**11. Is the problem this solves one you would switch tools for? What do you use today and what would make this better enough to switch?**

Yes, the problem is worth switching for. Today I keep Hudl next to a grading spreadsheet. I would switch when I can verify the actual cited snap, save my reason once, and have the coordinator reopen that same snap and correction during install on a different day.

**12. Would you choose to use this on your own time, unprompted? If not, why not?**

No. On my own Saturday I would have to keep the spreadsheet in case a cell switch or refresh erased the notes. I would use the six-snap blind drill with another coach as a training exercise.

**13. What single outcome would you measure to know it worked for you this season?**

I would measure the share of my Saturday handoffs that the coordinator can reopen on Monday at the right snap with my saved decision and reason, without asking me to reconstruct it.

## Learnings

**What worked**

In the six-snap exercise I held No Grade against a high-confidence Grade 1 until the combo assignment could be established. The revised practice focus correctly dropped play 2 and identified play 5 as its only contributor.

**What did not**

In the workspace the timestamped proxy did not show the cited Westmoor snap. Ordinary navigation discarded drafts, and a reload removed both the saved play 10 revision and the comment I had passed to the coordinator.

**What to try next**

Run a paired Saturday-to-Monday trial on one permissioned game. Have the position coach send corrected snaps, then have the coordinator reopen them in a fresh session. Count how many open on the original frame with the coach’s decision and reason intact.
