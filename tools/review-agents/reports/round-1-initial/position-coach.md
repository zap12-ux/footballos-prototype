**5. Where did you trust the AI's output, where did you not, and what made the difference?**

I trusted the record of my formation change because I could reopen it and see both values and my reason. On play 4 I kept No Grade even after the High-confidence Grade 1 appeared. The frame showed penetration, but it didn’t settle whose job that defender was.

**6. What would you stop using it over?**

I would stop signing off on labels if the only evidence I could reach was a different play. I’ll still flag the cut for someone who can open the original.

**7. What would you change first? Second?**

First, have the evidence link select the right play and show a frame I can check. Second, tell me which of the skipped labels nobody has reviewed before I accept anything in bulk.

**8. What should they not touch?**

Keep the blind-first grade, the words explaining what would make a snap gradable and the warning when a changed grade makes the practice focus stale. Those are decisions I need to make myself.

**9. Would you use this during the season? Would you recommend it to your staff? Answer yes, no or conditional, with the condition.**

Conditional for my own use and for a staff recommendation. I would pilot it alongside my sheet once the cited snap is reachable. I would recommend it to the staff when the queue works from the keyboard and the bulk counts distinguish untouched labels.

**10. Anything else.**

The focus named plays 2 and 5 and told me exactly which three snaps it left out. I could bring those two plays to a meeting. I can’t use this exercise to claim any number of hours saved.
