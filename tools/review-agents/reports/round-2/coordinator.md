## Interview answers

**1. First impression in one sentence.**

Clean, honest and half-finished: the plan side is something I could run a week on, the film side on my own unit's opponent gives me fractions with nothing behind them.

**2. Did the product match what the landing page led you to expect? Where not?**

Mostly. The landing page said tendencies read as fractions with the left-out snaps named, and they do, and it said unit plans go draft to coordinator approval and approved versions never change, and I watched v1 and v2 get superseded with a record. Where it fell short: it said software proposes play cuts and labels with evidence attached and that plans are proposals until a coach approves them. On Westmoor's defense every card says no supporting play, and there was no proposal of any kind to accept into the draft.

**3. Walk me through your normal week. Where would this fit, and where does it fight your routine?**

Saturday I tag our film in Hudl; here our Northgate film had no plays and no labels, so Saturday stays in Hudl. Sunday I put the opponent's fronts and pressures in a spreadsheet; the scouting page is that spreadsheet with denominators and basis badges, which fits, except I cannot click into a single Westmoor defensive snap. Monday through Wednesday I write the call sheet and hand the line coach his piece; the sections, the version comment and the approval record fit that exactly. Where it fights me: picking 'Their defense' flips my whole workspace to the defense plan, and my sidebar unit 'Offense' opens the opponent's offense, which is Priya's problem, not mine.

**4. What confused you? What did you have to figure out?**

Which unit the scouting page meant. 'Offense' in the sidebar is our unit everywhere else, and on scouting it became 'Their offense'. I also had to work out that the film list is not sorted by date and that the newest file is the one still processing. And on the playbook I had to figure out that there was no way to open page 4; I kept looking for a link.

**5. Where did you trust the AI's output, where did you not, and what made the difference?**

I trusted the labels on the Stonebrook film because every cell opened to the frames the model looked at, a sentence of reasoning, and a reason when it abstained; the down column never guessed. I trusted the offense findings that linked to a play, because the play I landed on carried the label status the link promised, including the 'Against' example. I did not trust the 9 of 11 run read because it is built on unreviewed labels, and it is already pasted into the defense plan as if it were settled. I could not trust or distrust the Westmoor defense cards at all: 14 of 22 with nothing to open is a number, not evidence.

**6. What would you stop using it over?**

If the opponent-defense findings still had no plays behind them by midseason, or if approving a plan kept happening in one click over an open question to my line coach.

**7. What would you change first? Second?**

First: a supporting play on every defensive and special-teams tendency, or do not show the card to me as something to plan from. Second: either give me the recommendation flow the landing page describes or stop promising it; right now the plan is typed by hand and the playbook concept I approved cannot even feed one.

**8. What should they not touch?**

The fraction-with-denominator and basis badge on every scouting card. The label panel with frames, rationale, abstention reasons and provenance. The version history line: approved by whom, when, superseded by what. The unsaved-changes handling on the draft; I tried to lose work twice and could not.

**9. Would you use this during the season? Would you recommend it to your staff? Answer yes, no or conditional, with the condition.**

Use: conditional. I would run the plan, comments and approval in season now, but I would not move my Sunday breakdown here until the opponent-defense tendencies open to a positive snap. Recommend: conditional, on the same thing plus a stop before approval when comments are open, because my staff will click Approve faster than I do.

**10. Anything else.**

The 'stand-in footage' label on the player is honest and I appreciate it, but a real season needs the real proxy; I judged the links on whether they chose the right play and label, not on what the clip showed. Personnel is read-only for me even though the contract says I can edit my packages, so the second-tight-end problem still lives in a comment.

**11. Is the problem this solves one you would switch tools for? What do you use today and what would make this better enough to switch?**

Yes, the Sunday-to-Wednesday chain is the problem I would switch for: today it is Hudl for film, a spreadsheet for fronts and pressures, and a shared document for the call sheet, and nothing connects a number to a snap to a section to an approval. This would be better enough to switch when a tendency on their defense opens to a play, the line coach can open that play from my Monday note, and the approved version is the one everybody prints.

**12. Would you choose to use this on your own time, unprompted? If not, why not?**

Not yet on my own time. I would open it to approve a plan or answer a comment because that is where the staff is, but I would not sit with it on a Sunday night while the scouting cards for my opponent have no plays, because I would just go back to the spreadsheet that does.

**13. What single outcome would you measure to know it worked for you this season?**

The share of Westmoor-week plan items whose claim I can open to a reviewed snap from inside the plan by Wednesday. This week that number is zero for the defensive tendencies and I typed the counts in by hand.

## Learnings

**What worked**

Probing retention three ways on the same draft (saved item, added-not-saved item, typed-only field) with distinctive demo text, then reloading, settled the round-1 loss findings OC-01 and OC-10 with before/after screenshots instead of inference.

**What did not**

Starting W1 from our own film found a blank timeline on the only 'Our film' asset, so the scenario's first two stages produced nothing to carry; the walkthrough could not test the handoff the persona cares most about, and the self-scout stage ran at zero evidence.

**What to try next**

Seed proposed plays and one positive evidence timestamp per defensive finding, then rerun this role's W1 and measure the persona's own season metric: the share of Westmoor-week plan items whose claim opens to a reviewed snap from inside the plan (zero this round).
