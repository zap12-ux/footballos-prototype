## Expectations

**Who is it for?** One high-school coaching staff on a private, invite-only team workspace: the head coach runs the team, coordinators approve unit plans, and everyone else sees what their role allows. It is not for players or parents and there is no public sign-up.

## Interview answers

**1. First impression in one sentence.**

It is the first thing that shows me a tendency as 19 of 54 with the plays it left out, and then lets me down the moment I click through to the play.

**2. Did the product match what the landing page led you to expect? Where not?**

Mostly. The landing page promised fractions with the snaps left out and why, coordinator approval and frozen approved versions, and I got all three: 31 of 44 with the left-out plays named, 'Approved Sat, Nov 21, 7:34 PM by Marcus Ellery', v1 marked superseded. It also promised that software proposes and the coach decides, and nothing proposed anything for my plan (C4 had no entry point); and it promised film cut into proposed plays, which our own Week 8 film does not have.

**3. Walk me through your normal week. Where would this fit, and where does it fight your routine?**

Saturday I tag our film; here our film has no plays, so Saturday stays in Hudl. Sunday I break down their fronts and pressures in a spreadsheet; the scouting page would replace that if the evidence links showed me the reviewed labels behind each count, which today they do not. Monday through Wednesday I build the call sheet and get Tomas's answers; the plan sections match my sheet, comments are on the version, and approval leaves a record, so that part fits. It fights me when scouting flips my unit to defense and Plans opens Priya's plan, and when an in-review version can only be approved.

**4. What confused you? What did you have to figure out?**

Why the review-basis switch added new findings instead of recomputing the even-front count. Why a reviewed-only finding points at play 5 where nothing is reviewed. Why the plan opened on Defense. Why my question to Tomas and Dana's hold vanished when I started v3. Why 'Ready' film has no plays. I figured each out by going back and reading, which is the same thing a coach on a Tuesday night will not do.

**5. Where did you trust the AI's output, where did you not, and what made the difference?**

I trusted the label panels: value, 'very unsure', the six frames it looked at with timestamps, the prompt hash, and the abstentions that say 'no down marker visible'; every one of the 96 I opened went to the right play. I trusted the play cuts for the same reason. I did not trust the scouting findings as support because 10:40 led to an unreviewed play with no front label, 4:10 led to a corrected 'doubles' under a trips claim, and two links led to plays with no labels at all. The difference was whether what I was shown on arrival was the thing the claim was built from.

**6. What would you stop using it over?**

If a count I taught from turned out to be built on plays nobody reviewed, or if a hold from the head coach dropped out of view when I started a new version and we installed over it.

**7. What would you change first? Second?**

First: make every evidence link open the play with the finding's labels and their review status shown, and only cite plays actually in the sample. Second: give our own film plays, or let me cut them, so Saturday starts here.

**8. What should they not touch?**

The fraction-and-left-out format on every finding, 'No supporting play on file' instead of a dead link, the stand-in notice that names the play and second, the label panel's frames and provenance, the approval record with superseded-by, and the way my unsaved line waited for me after I went to the dashboard.

**9. Would you use this during the season? Would you recommend it to your staff? Answer yes, no or conditional, with the condition.**

Use: conditional. Recommend: conditional. The condition is the same for both: evidence links on reviewed-only findings must land on plays that show the reviewed labels behind the count, and our own film must have play cuts, because without those the Sunday breakdown and the Saturday self-scout both stay in the tools I already use.

**10. Anything else.**

The honesty is the best thing here: it says the clip is a stand-in, it says 'too small to call', it says 'no supporting play on file'. Keep that register and fix what it is being honest about.

**11. Is the problem this solves one you would switch tools for? What do you use today and what would make this better enough to switch?**

Yes, the Sunday breakdown is the job I would switch for. Today it is Hudl for tags and a spreadsheet for fronts and pressures, then a call sheet in a document. Better enough means: one click from a count to the reviewed snaps it came from, both review bases on the same tendency, and the plan line linked back to that count. The sheet and approval loop is already better than my document.

**12. Would you choose to use this on your own time, unprompted? If not, why not?**

Not yet. On my own time I open film, and here our film is empty. I would open it unprompted on a Sunday if the opponent findings held up when I clicked through.

**13. What single outcome would you measure to know it worked for you this season?**

Number of install-plan lines that are backed by a scouting count whose cited snaps I checked and found labeled as claimed. If that is most of the sheet by Wednesday every week, it worked.

## Keep list

- Every scouting finding prints numerator, denominator, percentage and sample, with 'Left out (n)' reasons and the review basis as a tag; a finding without a play says 'No supporting play on file' instead of linking nowhere, and contrary examples stay under 'Against'.
- Every seek in the film player states 'stand-in footage for m:ss (play N, start–end)' and that the credited clip is not the snap; all 48 cuts and all 96 model cells landed on the right play in the audit.
- The label correction panel shows the model value with an uncertainty word, the rationale, the 'Looked at' span, the frames inspected as clickable timestamps, model, prompt hash, cost and latency, and abstentions explain what was not visible ('down is never inferred from play context').
- Unsaved plan edits survive leaving to another page and coming back, and saved edits survive reload and version switching; the version select shows exactly one approved (current) version.
- Approval history: 'Approved <time> by <name>' on the approved version and 'Superseded <time> by vN (approved by <name>)' on the superseded one, never cleared.
- Plan sections are exactly the CONTRACTS §11 offense template, and comments are tagged to a section and bound to a version.
- Head-coach-only controls are disabled with the reason in plain text ('Only the head coach can change retention', 'Only the head coach can invite staff') rather than failing after submission.
- The demo environment label on every screen and the explicit 'Demo proxy' and 'authored' notes keep the mock from passing for a real result.

## Verdict

**Would use** conditional

**Would recommend** conditional

Use and recommendation both depend on two fixes: evidence links on reviewed-only findings must open plays that show the reviewed labels the count was built from (the 10:40, 4:10, 32:00 and 55:34 links did not), and our own game film must have play cuts or a way to make them (the Week 8 Hawks film is 'Ready' with none). With those, the Sunday breakdown and the Saturday self-scout move into this tool; the plan, comment and approval loop is already usable and I would keep it as is.

## Learnings

**What worked**

Walking every evidence path one by one rather than sampling: all 48 cuts and all 96 label cells reached their play, which let the review say exactly where trust breaks (the scouting-to-play links) instead of guessing from a few clicks.

**What did not**

Running W1 in the same session after C1–C7: the unit context left over from scouting opened the wrong plan twice, and the self-scout stage could not start because the seed gives our own film no segments, so the connected week was rated against a fixture gap as much as against the product.

**What to try next**

Before the next round, seed segments and a few reviewed labels on hawks_vs_northgate_sideline.mp4 and make each finding's evidence refs point only at plays in includedSegmentIds; then re-run W1 and measure the Q13 outcome directly: how many v3 plan lines have a cited snap whose labels match the claim (target: every line that cites a count).
