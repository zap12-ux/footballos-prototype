**1. First impression in one sentence.**

I can get from a queue to a review decision, but the moment I need the guard’s first two steps the workspace plays another snap.

**2. Did the product match what the landing page led you to expect? Where not?**

I found cuts, corrections and the weekly plan the landing described. The timestamp was honest about the stand-in, but I had expected to be able to check the frame itself.

**3. Walk me through your normal week. Where would this fit, and where does it fight your routine?**

Saturday night I grade Hudl beside a sheet and pull clips for the Monday line period. The proposed queue could start that job, and the practice-focus exclusions make sense on Sunday. Re-entering a filename, cut number and reason in a plan comment makes the handoff a second job.

**4. What confused you? What did you have to figure out?**

Pressing A selected the next cut, so I thought R would reject that one. It did nothing until I clicked the list again. In the Personnel dialog, I also had to reconcile forty “already reviewed” with the empty cells below.

**5. Where did you trust the AI's output, where did you not, and what made the difference?**

I trusted the Play 3 abstention because the authored explanation identified 62 versus 66 and the missing view at contact. I kept my blind No Grade on 4. Penetration and two High readings could not tell me whose linebacker that was.

**6. What would you stop using it over?**

I would stop if my staff treated a play clock and an illustrative clip as the original rep, then put the grade beside a kid’s number.

**7. What would you change first? Second?**

First, let me open the exact game snap from the label. Second, carry that snap and my reason into the coordinator’s plan so he can reopen it without copying identifiers.

**8. What should they not touch?**

Keep the original proposal beside my corrected entry and the dated revision reason. Keep the blind-first play and the warning when a saved focus loses a contributor.

**9. Would you use this during the season? Would you recommend it to your staff? Answer yes, no or conditional, with the condition.**

I would use it during the season and recommend it to the staff only if a correction can travel with its actual snap into the coordinator’s plan. Today I would keep my current grading sheet.

**10. Anything else.**

My synthetic color-and-clock file turned into a football clip in the player. The stand-in caption is important, especially where a coach could otherwise take the film as proof.

**11. Is the problem this solves one you would switch tools for? What do you use today and what would make this better enough to switch?**

I would switch from Hudl plus the grading sheet for a Saturday-to-Monday workflow where the coordinator can reopen the exact cut, see my reason and keep the original proposal beside it.

**12. Would you choose to use this on your own time, unprompted? If not, why not?**

I would revisit the six-play exercise on my own to discuss why No Grade is fair. I would not do a week of player grading in the current workspace on my own time because I would have to verify the video elsewhere.

**13. What single outcome would you measure to know it worked for you this season?**

I would measure the fraction of Saturday OL corrections that the coordinator opens on the correct original snap from Monday’s unit plan without asking me to find it.

## Learnings

**What worked**

Switching away from a pending formation and returning restored both fields. Its saved reason later appeared in revision history.

**What did not**

The source timestamp played illustrative footage, and posting the correction in the plan required entering the film reference again.

**What to try next**

With a real source-enabled test fixture, have the coordinator open each Saturday correction from the Monday plan and count how many land on the correct original snap without the position coach locating it again.
