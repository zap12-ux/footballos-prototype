## Interview answers

**1. First impression in one sentence.**

A film tool that cuts, labels and keeps every change with a name on it, with a separate little demo that shows what grading a lineman would look like but isn't hooked up to my film yet.

**2. Did the product match what the landing page led you to expect? Where not?**

Mostly. The landing page promised proposed cuts and labels with evidence attached and coaches owning every decision, and that's what Our Team Film does: cuts with timestamps, labels with the frames the model looked at, my corrections kept with my name. Where it didn't match: the page says 'game film to game plan' as one loop, but the grading part of my job, a grade on No. 62 with a reason, only exists in the prototype demo on six authored snaps; the real film screen labels plays, it doesn't grade players. And the newest film in the list was still processing, so the first thing I opened had nothing to review.

**3. Walk me through your normal week. Where would this fit, and where does it fight your routine?**

Saturday night I cut and grade; Sunday I put a coaching point in front of the OC; Tuesday I run it in indy. The film screen fits Saturday: queue, A to accept, click a cell, fix a label, reason, save. The plan fits Sunday: I added the point to Runs and left a comment for the OC on the same screen. It fights my routine in two places: every accept drops my keyboard out of the list, so the next snap needs a mouse click, and the plan held my item as 'Unsaved changes' until I found Save draft. Also, the film I uploaded never went into the review queue, so N did nothing on fresh film.

**4. What confused you? What did you have to figure out?**

Three things. Why 'One tile per 10 s' shows tiles four and a half minutes apart. Why the player said play 5 at 10:35 while the list still said play 33 selected after I followed an evidence link. And in the demo, after I rewrote the focus and then changed a grade, where my wording went; the notice told me it was set aside, but I had to work out that it wasn't coming back on its own.

**5. Where did you trust the AI's output, where did you not, and what made the difference?**

I trusted the label proposals where the link landed on the right second of the right play and the panel told me what it looked at and why, like play 5 run with 'handoff visible at frame 3'. I didn't trust the Play 4 Grade 1 in the demo even at High/High, because its own uncertainty list said it couldn't tell whose defender came through, and I didn't trust the two scouting claims that said 'No supporting play on file'. The difference was whether I could get to a frame in one or two clicks, not the confidence word.

**6. What would you stop using it over?**

If the keyboard kept letting go of the list after every accept on a 60-cut night, or if the plan ever dropped a point I'd typed. Tonight nothing was lost; the unsaved item came back after a reload with a warning on it, which is the right behaviour, but if that ever went quiet I'd be out.

**7. What would you change first? Second?**

First, keep focus on the play list after A and R so the next key moves to the next cut, and put fresh uploads' cuts in the review queue so N works on new film. Second, keep the grid filter I was looking at after Accept inferred labels in view, and stop calling empty no-model rows 'already reviewed' in that confirmation.

**8. What should they not touch?**

The correction panel: model value, rationale, Looked-at link, my value, my reason, my name and time, history. The bulk-accept confirmation that names the count, the plays and what it skips and never takes an abstention. The pending-not-saved draft that survived me walking off to the plan and back. And the Play 4 blind-first gate in the demo.

**9. Would you use this during the season? Would you recommend it to your staff? Answer yes, no or conditional, with the condition.**

Conditional on both. I'd use the film screen in season if A and R keep my place in the list and new film lands in the queue; and I'd want the per-snap player grade with a reason from the demo to exist on my real film before I'd call it my grading tool. I'd recommend it to the staff on the same condition, because the labels-with-evidence and the plan hand-off already work.

**10. Anything else.**

The reason box on the real film screen is free text; the demo has a reason list in my words (hand placement, combo timing). Put that list on the film screen, so three coaches say the same thing the same way. And the scouting page is honest when it has no supporting play, which I respect, but a tendency with no play behind it shouldn't sit at the same size as one with two.

**11. Is the problem this solves one you would switch tools for? What do you use today and what would make this better enough to switch?**

The problem it solves, cuts and labels I can check and a point that reaches the OC without a text message, is one I'd switch for. Today it's Hudl on one side and a spreadsheet on the other. It gets better enough to switch when the grade I give a lineman on a snap lives on this screen with the snap, and the keyboard loop is clean; right now the grading half is still a demo.

**12. Would you choose to use this on your own time, unprompted? If not, why not?**

The film screen, yes, on a Saturday night without being told, because it's faster to A through cuts and fix labels than to retype them. The demo, no; it's six authored snaps and after one pass there's nothing new to look at.

**13. What single outcome would you measure to know it worked for you this season?**

Coaching points that reach Tuesday practice with the snap attached: count how many of my Sunday points on the offense plan carry a play reference the OC can open in one click, and how many snaps I graded per hour with the keyboard only. If both go up across the season, it worked.

## Keep list

- Label correction panel on /app/film/:assetId: model value and rationale, 'Looked at' evidence link, inspected frames with clickable timestamps, 'In use' value with reviewer name and time, free-text reason kept in a visible revision history (Inferred → Corrected with the reason verbatim).
- Pending label correction survives switching cells and navigating away and back, shown as 'Pending, not saved' with a Discard control; saved corrections survive a reload with reviewer and reason.
- 'Accept inferred labels in view' confirmation that states the count, the plays and the key, lists what is skipped, never accepts an abstention (Down view shows '(0)' and is disabled), and leaves filtered-out keys untouched.
- Honest seek refusals and stand-in labelling on the demo proxy: 'Footage for 0:10 is not in this proxy' and 'stand-in footage for m:ss (play N, start–end)' on every evidence seek.
- Scouting findings print numerator, denominator, 'Left out (n)' reasons, the review basis on each finding, evidence links that land on the cited second of the cited play, and 'No supporting play on file' instead of a dead link.
- Role boundaries as hidden affordances with a sentence of explanation: no approve control on an in-review plan for a position coach, retention disabled with 'Only the head coach can change retention', staff with no controls.
- Prototype trust mechanics: two separate confidence readings with plain meanings, Play 4 blind-first with both grades stored, required structured reasons for a change and for No Grade, 'What would make this gradable', practice focus listing its plays, exclusions and 'Logged, not treated as a deficiency', stale-and-rebuild after a decision change, demo export with mode 'demo' and baseline null.
- Plan comments post immediately and read back after a reload with author, time and section; the unsaved plan item is restored after a reload with an explicit 'Unsaved changes' notice rather than dropped.

## Verdict

**Would use** conditional

**Would recommend** conditional

Keep keyboard focus on the play list after A and R (and after Enter) so the next key moves to the next cut; put a fresh upload's proposed cuts into the review queue as soon as processing finishes so N works on new film without a round trip; and bring the per-snap player grade with a structured reason from the demo onto the real film screen. With the first two fixed I would run Saturday cuts and labels on it this season; the third is what makes it my grading tool rather than my labelling tool.

## Learnings

**What worked**

Driving the whole session through real keyboard events on the play list exposed the one thing a mouse-only walk would have hidden: every A or R drops focus to <body>, so the second key of a two-key rhythm is lost (P1, W1). Running W1 on film I had uploaded myself, rather than the seeded asset, showed that fresh cuts never enter the queue while a coach-merged cut does.

**What did not**

The demo session could not be re-entered from its results screen to re-check evidence, so the audit needed a second demo session in the same profile; and the headless driver's script clicks meant mouse-click focus placement and the Download Session JSON file could not be observed, so those are recorded as unverified rather than as product behaviour.

**What to try next**

Next Saturday session: upload one disposable asset, then grade 20 cuts with keyboard only and count how many times focus has to be recovered with the mouse (target zero after a fix) and whether 'Next in queue' is non-zero on the fresh film; then hand two points to the plan and check whether each carries an openable play reference, which is the season measure named in Q13.
