## Interview answers

**1. First impression in one sentence.**

It is an honest, well-labelled workspace that already does the approval trail better than our planning sheet, but it stops short of the two things a head coach gets asked about most: who can see this, and can I take that film down.

**2. Did the product match what the landing page led you to expect? Where not?**

Mostly yes. The landing page said film to plan with the coach in charge, and the plans, scouting counts with denominators and the override trail delivered that. It also promised 'expiring media links' and 'Staff — roles by unit, invitations, retention settings', and the links part is not there: no share control on any plan (H5), and retention is a number with no explanation of what it does to existing film (H2). I expected to open film with proposed plays in ten minutes and did, though the most recent upload was still processing.

**3. Walk me through your normal week. Where would this fit, and where does it fight your routine?**

Saturday morning film comes in, Sunday I check who owes what before the staff meeting, Monday the install goes out, Tuesday and Wednesday are practice scripts, Thursday is the walkthrough. The dashboard and the Version list on the plans fit the Sunday check; the plan override fits Sunday night when a coordinator is unreachable. It fights my routine when a scouting finding has to be retyped into the plan by hand (H4), when the dashboard's open-task count changed under me without anyone deciding anything (W1), and when I have to go to Staff to find out which coordinator owes the approval.

**4. What confused you? What did you have to figure out?**

Three things. The dashboard said 3 play cuts were waiting after my test upload when 16 Westmoor cuts were still in the queue, and its link took me to scouting rather than the film; I had to open the asset to trust the number. After I clicked an evidence link inside a label panel the playhead moved but 'play 32 selected' stayed on the right, so I had to read the status line to know what Accept would act on. And the thumbnail strip says one tile per 10 seconds while the labels under the tiles are four and a half minutes apart.

**5. Where did you trust the AI's output, where did you not, and what made the difference?**

I trusted the label panels and the scouting findings that carried a timestamp, because each one opened the cited play and told me honestly that the demo clip is a stand-in. I trusted the abstentions more than the values: 'no down marker visible, down is never inferred' is exactly what I would want it to say. I did not trust the findings that read 'No supporting play on file' (the defense fifth-rusher and corner findings, both special-teams findings) and I would not plan around them; nor the playbook OCR candidates, because nothing lets me see page 4. The difference was whether I could get to a snap in one click.

**6. What would you stop using it over?**

If the open-task list keeps changing its counts or dropping items the way the offense comment vanished from it after a new draft, I would stop trusting it as my Sunday list, and that is the only reason I would open it on a Sunday. And if film cannot be deleted, I cannot keep the promise I make to parents about footage of their kids.

**7. What would you change first? Second?**

First: sharing and deletion, together, because they are the two questions I get from parents and the AD. Second: put the owner's name on every open task and on an in-review plan, and fix the cut count and its link, so the Sunday check answers 'who' without a detour to Staff.

**8. What should they not touch?**

The override panel and the version lines. Approved by, superseded by, and my reason quoted back on the version is exactly right. Keep the draft keeping my unsaved text across navigation and reload, keep the 'includes unreviewed labels' flag on every count, and keep 'No supporting play on file' instead of a fake link.

**9. Would you use this during the season? Would you recommend it to your staff? Answer yes, no or conditional, with the condition.**

Use it: conditional. I would run the plans and the approval trail this season once a plan can be shared with an expiry and a wrong upload can be deleted; until then it is a staff-only tool I would use alongside Hudl, not instead of it. Recommend to staff: conditional on the same two fixes plus owners on the Sunday list, because the first thing an assistant will ask me is who is waiting on them.

**10. Anything else.**

The demo is upfront about what is authored and what is a stand-in, which made the review fair. The prototype's No Grade screen is the clearest explanation of an abstention I have seen; the label panels in the main app are heading the same way. One small thing: the override panel on the defense plan talks about superseding 'the current approved version' when there is none.

**11. Is the problem this solves one you would switch tools for? What do you use today and what would make this better enough to switch?**

The problem it solves for me is the approval trail and the pending list for a game week; today that is a shared sheet, texts and Hudl's film. I would switch for the plan and approval side if a plan could leave the building safely (share with expiry, revoke) and if an assistant could be onboarded and removed without me explaining it. Film cutting and labels would not pull me off Hudl yet; the cuts here are even slices and labels stopped at 12 of 48 plays.

**12. Would you choose to use this on your own time, unprompted? If not, why not?**

On my own time, unprompted, I would open the plans page on a Sunday night to see what is in review, because that is a real itch and it answered it in two clicks. I would not open the film side unprompted until the cuts are real snaps and the queue count on the dashboard can be trusted.

**13. What single outcome would you measure to know it worked for you this season?**

The number of Sunday-night texts I send asking a coordinator where their plan is. If the dashboard and the Version list get that to zero for a month of game weeks, it worked.

## Keep list

- Plans Version list: the approval line ('Approved <date> by <name> · head-coach override: “reason”') and the superseded line ('Superseded <date> by vN (approved by <name>, head-coach override)') stay on the version after leaving and reopening; keep the reason quoted verbatim.
- 'Approve (override)' panel: the Confirm button stays disabled until a reason is typed, and Cancel leaves the version in review.
- Draft plans keep added items and even half-typed Add-field text across navigation, unit switching and reload, with the 'Unsaved changes · N Add field not added' status line.
- Every scouting count shows its denominator, the review-basis flag, the left-out plays with reasons, and 'No supporting play on file' instead of a dead link; contrary examples stay under 'Against'.
- Label panels show the model's rationale, the exact frames inspected with clickable timestamps, provenance and cost; abstentions explain what was not visible and are never bulk-accepted.
- Film player: every seek says which play the stand-in footage stands for and never seeks silently to a time no play covers; scouting ?t= links select the cited play.
- Dashboard and every link carry team, season, game and unit; context was never lost in the session.
- Staff invitation form: four fields, one button, the row appears immediately with role, unit and expiry; the head coach's own row cannot be deactivated.
- Prototype demo: candidate, two separate confidence readings with plain-language meanings, evidence frames and 'What would make this gradable' on one screen, with the simulated/illustration disclosures.

## Verdict

**Would use** conditional

**Would recommend** conditional

I would run the weekly plans and the approval trail in season once an approved plan can be shared with a visible expiry and revoke, and a wrong upload can be deleted by the head coach; until then it stays a staff-only tool beside Hudl. Recommendation to staff is conditional on the same two fixes plus owners on the Sunday open-task list.

## Learnings

**What worked**

Treating the override as a first-class, reasoned action worked: the head coach could approve v2 and v3 without a coordinator, and both versions and the superseded v1 told the full story afterwards (H3, H4), which is the trace the AD conversation needs.

**What did not**

The Sunday check broke on 'who' and on counts: no open task or in-review version names a person, the cut count dropped from 16 to 3 after an unrelated upload and linked to scouting, and the parent-access question had no screen to answer it because sharing and deletion are not exposed (H5, H6, W1).

**What to try next**

Ship a share control with expiry and revoke, a Delete on the asset view, and owner names on the dashboard and in-review versions; then rerun W1 and measure whether all three Sunday questions are answered from the dashboard without opening Staff, and whether the persona's Sunday-night 'where is your plan' texts go to zero over a month of game weeks.
