## Interview answers

**1. First impression in one sentence.**

I could see the Westmoor game, the defense approval and the open comments before I had to open a single film.

**2. Did the product match what the landing page led you to expect? Where not?**

I expected film evidence and a coordinator approval trail. The versions gave me the trail. The run label opened a credited substitute, and I could not find a plan-sharing control on the version I approved.

**3. Walk me through your normal week. Where would this fit, and where does it fight your routine?**

Sunday I check my seven assistants’ outstanding decisions, agree the install with the coordinators, then make sure the right people can see it. The dashboard and defense v1 start that conversation. I would keep the shared planning sheet for owners and the existing access system for a parent request.

**4. What confused you? What did you have to figure out?**

I saw Draft saved beside an unadded Short yardage line. Switching units brought my text back, so I thought it would be there after Submit for review. The approved section was empty.

**5. Where did you trust the AI's output, where did you not, and what made the difference?**

I trusted that the head-coach reason had been recorded because I reopened the version and read it. Play 5 called a run and cited a handoff, but the player told me the credited clip was a stand-in. I would need the Westmoor snap before using that call with an assistant.

**6. What would you stop using it over?**

I would stop using it as our plan of record if ordinary submission let a typed coaching point disappear. If we upload identifiable film, I also need to be able to act on a removal request.

**7. What would you change first? Second?**

I would first make Submit flag every unadded entry and let me include it. Second, I would give the staff an approved version with an audience, expiry and revocation I can verify before sending it.

**8. What should they not touch?**

I would keep the original approval on a superseded version, the separate head-coach reason and the date on each. I would also keep the honest warning that the clip is a stand-in.

**9. Would you use this during the season? Would you recommend it to your staff? Answer yes, no or conditional, with the condition.**

I would use it conditionally, once submitting a plan accounts for my pending entries and we can trace and control the version we distribute. I would recommend it to the staff conditionally, after I can show a real snap behind a call and verify film removal and plan access.

**10. Anything else.**

I could invite one defensive assistant and still see the role, unit and expiry after reload. That is a piece of staff administration I would trust someone to learn on their first Sunday.

**11. Is the problem this solves one you would switch tools for? What do you use today and what would make this better enough to switch?**

I would consider switching from Hudl, our planning sheet and the school calendar for a single trace from Sunday film to Monday’s approved version. I still had to copy the 9-of-11 finding by hand, and the approved sentence had no route back.

**12. Would you choose to use this on your own time, unprompted? If not, why not?**

I would open the dashboard on my own to see which unit is in review. I would follow up with the coordinator outside it before treating the Sunday check as finished.

**13. What single outcome would you measure to know it worked for you this season?**

I would measure the share of Monday installs for which every pending coaching point is accounted for in the approved version before staff receive it. I would set a target of 100 percent for a four-game trial.
